Cultural Barriers in Accessing Healthcare for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander People

Acknowledgement of Country This project acknowledges Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples as the Traditional Custodians of the lands across Australia, and pays respect to Elders past and present.

📘 Overview

This academic project investigates how cultural, linguistic, historical, and systemic factors influence healthcare access for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in Australia.
The goal is to identify barriers, highlight community perspectives, and propose culturally safe strategies for improving health outcomes.

🎯 Research Objectives
Examine cultural and communication barriers in healthcare settings.
Analyse the impact of historical and social determinants on healthcare access.
Review existing models of culturally safe care.
Provide recommendations for policy, practice, and community-led solutions.
❓ Research Question

What cultural barriers affect, and what cultural enablers facilitate, access to healthcare for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander populations in Australia?

📄 Abstract

Objective:

To identify the cultural barriers affecting access to healthcare for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander populations in Australia.
To identify the cultural enablers that facilitate access to healthcare for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander populations in Australia.

Introduction: Despite comprising 2.3% of Australia's population, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people face significant challenges accessing necessary healthcare facilities. Cultural barriers often impede access, leading to disparities in health outcomes. Understanding these cultural challenges and identifying enablers are crucial for improving the health status of these communities.

Inclusion criteria: The review includes studies focused on young adults and adults above 14 years identified as Indigenous Australians. Studies must address both cultural challenges and enablers in accessing healthcare facilities, including public care, hospitals, and specialist centers.

Research outcomes:

The research outcomes of this scoping review will provide a comprehensive understanding of the cultural factors influencing healthcare access for Australian Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples.
By examining barriers, enablers, and disparities in healthcare access, the review aims to identify key areas for intervention and improvement in healthcare delivery and policy.

Methods: This scoping review was conducted in accordance with the JBI methodology for scoping reviews and is reported as per the PRISMA-ScR checklist. The review title was prospectively registered in the Open Science Framework (osf.io/3h5pe). A three-step search strategy was used to locate published and unpublished studies in MEDLINE (Ovid), Scopus, Cochrane Library, CINAHL (EBSCO), Web of Science, and Embase, plus grey literature sources (ProQuest, Google Scholar, and Informit). English-language studies published from inception to May 2024 were included. Citations were collated in EndNote 21 and duplicates removed. Titles and abstracts were screened against the inclusion criteria after a pilot test of 10 records, and full texts were assessed in detail by two or more independent reviewers using JBI SUMARI, with disagreements resolved by a third reviewer. Results are presented in a PRISMA flow diagram.

Keywords: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health, cultural safety, healthcare access, health equity, social determinants of health, community-led care

🧠 Background & Significance

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples experience significant disparities in healthcare access due to cultural differences, institutional bias, and historical trauma.
Understanding these barriers is essential for designing culturally safe, community-informed healthcare systems.

Key contexts for this work include:

Cultural and communication factors: language, health literacy, communication style, and the use of interpreters or Aboriginal Health Workers.
Historical and social determinants: the ongoing effects of colonisation, past experiences with services, and socioeconomic factors.
Systemic factors: service availability, cost, distance, workforce diversity, and the cultural safety of clinical environments.
Community-controlled health: the role of Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Services in providing culturally safe care.

🔎 Search strategy

The search aimed to locate both published and unpublished studies, using a three-step search strategy:

Initial limited search of MEDLINE (Ovid) and Scopus to identify articles on the topic.
Full search strategy: the text words in the titles and abstracts of relevant articles, and the index terms used to describe them, were used to develop a full search strategy for MEDLINE (Ovid) (see Appendix I). This strategy, including all identified keywords and index terms, is adapted for each included database and information source.
Reference list screening: the reference lists of all included sources of evidence are screened for additional studies after full-text screening.

Databases: MEDLINE (Ovid), Scopus, Cochrane Library, CINAHL (EBSCO), Web of Science, Embase

Grey literature: ProQuest, Google Scholar, Informit

Appendix I: full MEDLINE (Ovid) search strategy, available in docs/.

🧾 Screening and selection
Collate and deduplicate: following the search, all identified citations are collated and uploaded into EndNote version 21 (Clarivate Analytics, PA, USA) and duplicates are removed.
Title and abstract screening: after a pilot test of 10 records, titles and abstracts are screened by reviewers against the inclusion criteria for the review.
Full-text retrieval: potentially relevant sources are retrieved in full, and their citation details are imported into the JBI System for the Unified Management, Assessment and Review of Information (JBI SUMARI) (JBI, Adelaide, Australia).
Full-text assessment: the full text of selected citations is assessed in detail against the inclusion criteria by two or more independent reviewers.
Exclusions: reasons for exclusion of sources of evidence at full text that do not meet the inclusion criteria are recorded and reported in the scoping review report.
Disagreements: any disagreements between reviewers at each stage of the selection process are resolved by a third reviewer.
Reporting: the results of the search and the study inclusion process are reported in full in the final scoping review and presented in a PRISMA flow diagram.

⚖️ Ethical Considerations
Research with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and Communities

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander research includes all research that impacts on or is of particular significance to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, including the planning, collection, analysis and dissemination of information or knowledge, in any format or medium, which is about, or may affect, Indigenous peoples, either collectively or individually.

Ethical approval is not applicable to this scoping review as it involves secondary analysis of existing literature. Therefore, issues such as consent, confidentiality, and data protection do not apply.

Conflicts of interest will be declared, and publication ethics will be maintained throughout the review process.
Sensitive topics involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples will be handled with respect and in accordance with ethical guidelines (NS 4.7).
Transparency and thorough reporting will be ensured, and the study's potential impact and funding sources will be disclosed.
